# Investigations in Learning Analytics: Bridging Gaps in Higher Education through Data Insights

**Researcher:** Elisabet Tammjarv  
**Institution:** University of Glasgow, School of Computing Science  
**Supervisor:** Alistair Morrison  

## Project Overview

This research project explores the implementation and implications of Learning Analytics (LA) in higher education through a novel participatory design methodology. The study investigates student perspectives, ethical considerations, and theoretical frameworks for effective institute-wide LA systems. In this repository is the accompanying code for the research project.

## Research Questions

1. **What insights** can we garner from past research and current student perspectives for institute-wide LA implementation?
2. **How can institutes** best navigate the complex ethical and legal landscape surrounding analytics?
3. **What are the major theoretical factors** involved in successful development and implementation of LA systems?


## Participatory Design Approach
This research introduces an innovative methodology addressing the gap in student-centered LA development:


### Research Process
1. **Initial one-on-one interviews** to gather individual perspectives
2. **Collaborative prototype development** as an information-gathering device
3. **Iterative design cycles** with participant feedback
4. **Longitudinal tracking** of changing perceptions and insights

## 📈 Key Findings

### Student Perspectives
- **Privacy concerns** are paramount regarding personal data commodification
- **Human interaction preference** over automated systems
- **Diverse needs** across student population
- **Growing acceptance** with increased LA understanding

### Theoretical Factors Identified
- **Trust dynamics** in institutional systems and code
- **Artificial motivation** considerations
- **Unique educational aspects** distinguishing LA from other analytics
- **Fairness questions** in algorithmic decision-making
- **Behaviorism vs. constructivism** tensions
- **Surveillance capitalism** concerns
- **Data autonomy** and self-evaluation importance
- **Human connection preservation** despite technological changes

## Conclusion

### Positive Outcomes
- **Participatory design** successfully addressed ethical concerns
- **Student perceptions shifted positively** through iterative engagement
- **Methodology demonstrated potential** for enhancing LA acceptability

### Critical Recommendation
Developing a **positive institute culture** surrounding LA is essential, particularly given its inevitable role in future higher education.


*This research represents a comprehensive investigation into the human, technical, and ethical dimensions of Learning Analytics in modern higher education.*
